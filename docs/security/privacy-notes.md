# Privacy Notes

Hairline may eventually handle client contact details, appointment records, staff information, customer hair history, photos, reviews, identity-related comfort filters, and business data. Treat those as private by default when data models and integrations are introduced.

## Sensitive Data Categories

- Client contact details.
- Appointment history.
- Hair type, texture, style preferences, and photo history.
- Reviews and review requests.
- Queer-friendly, gender-affirming, accessibility, language, and other comfort/safety filters.
- Professional CRM notes.
- Marketing consent records.

## Consent Rules

Photos captured after a service belong in the customer's private history by default.

Professional marketing use requires explicit customer consent. Consent should be specific to the asset, auditable, and revocable.

The app must not treat booking, payment, review submission, or photo receipt as implied marketing consent.

## Professional CRM Privacy

Professional-side customer records should be scoped to the professional or business account that created the relationship. If a professional changes salons, architecture must carefully separate the professional's legitimate client relationship from any salon-owned operational data.

## Matching Privacy

Matching should use customer-provided hair and preference data in ways that improve recommendations without exposing unnecessary sensitive profile details to professionals or other users.
